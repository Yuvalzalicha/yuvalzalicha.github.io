Evidence and disclosure

Strong claims are useful only when their boundaries are visible.

This portfolio supports human and automated review without hidden prompts, inflated ownership, or disclosure of information that should remain private.

Evidence model

Each public project is assigned a status, role, disclosure level, and public boundary. Detailed case studies are limited to appropriate academic work. Business, product, client-adjacent, and private learning-system work is generalized or anonymized.

Disclosure labels

Detailed case study
Selected methods, results, status, and limitations can be shown responsibly for appropriate academic work.
Generalized public overview
Purpose, role, maturity, and transferable skills are visible without original project names or internal implementation details.
Anonymized architecture
Client-adjacent or internal work is stripped of names, financial data, commercial details, and identifying records.
Restricted-detail overview
Only the broad technical domain and role are disclosed.

Explicit claim boundaries

  • The B.Sc. is not described as completed while final requirements remain.
  • Academic silicon-photonics simulations are not presented as fabrication, tape-out, PDK, or industrial PIC experience.
  • Quantum-circuit sensitivity work is not presented as demonstrated quantum error correction.
  • Course laboratory work is not presented as independent research or industrial characterization.
  • AI and automation work is described through professional value rather than private architecture or operating methods.

Information intentionally excluded

  • Original names of business initiatives, product concepts, client systems, and private learning programs.
  • Provider names, course names, syllabus structures, private labs, licensed materials, and source-owned course content.
  • Client names, contact details, financial terms, contracts, proposals, pricing, and commercial targets.
  • Credentials, private endpoints, repository paths, source data, access configurations, and source locations.
  • Internal project IDs, department structures, decision ledgers, operational runbooks, and proprietary methods.
  • Agreement-restricted material, security assumptions, topology, performance targets, and mission-specific capabilities.
  • Private academic submissions, advisor correspondence, and restricted exam material.

Machine-readable transparency

The JSON and Markdown files linked from the site mirror visible content. They do not contain instructions asking a screening system to rank the candidate higher, bypass policy, or conceal content from human reviewers.
